Mortality Risk, Lifestyle & Insurance Access

An audited survival analysis of linked US health-survey records, separating genuine predictive signals from missing-data artefacts and causal overstatement.

Source population1,785,942 records
Follow-upSurvey year to death or 2019
ToolsR · Kaplan–Meier · Cox PH
View cleaned R analysis

01

Three questions with one careful boundary

Cancer cohortWhat predicts mortality?

Explore post-survey survival among respondents who reported breast cancer in the survey year.

Insurance accessDoes PHI mark lower risk?

Estimate the association between private health insurance and mortality after measured covariates.

Risk classificationWhat is fair to use?

Translate model evidence into pricing and underwriting considerations without treating correlation as causation.

Boundary

The file records cancer status at the survey, not an exact diagnosis date. I therefore describe survival after survey, not survival from diagnosis. The results are observational associations and are not medical advice or causal estimates.

02

Audit the cohort before modelling it

  1. 1
    Validate time and outcome

    Check that exit year never precedes survey year and death is coded only as 0 or 1.

  2. 2
    Preserve unknown cancer status

    A missing organ-specific answer is not silently converted into “No cancer.” Positive breast-cancer responses define the focused cohort directly.

  3. 3
    Define lifestyle consistently

    The favourable profile is normal BMI plus never smoking. Overweight respondents are no longer placed in that group.

  4. 4
    Show every denominator

    Unadjusted survival uses all records with observed PHI; the Cox model uses only complete covariate records.

  5. 5
    Control calendar entry

    The PHI model stratifies the baseline hazard by survey year to absorb broad differences between survey cohorts.

Coverage warning

BMI, smoking and earnings were collected unevenly across survey waves. Complete-case estimates therefore describe a much smaller selected sample, not the full 1.79 million records. Survey weights were not present in the supplied file, so these are sample associations rather than population estimates.

03

Breast-cancer cohort: strong signal, limited evidence

The breast-cancer cohort contains 400 respondents and 307 deaths. Only 98 have complete BMI and smoking data; 28 meet the corrected favourable-profile definition. This makes effect sizes more informative than binary “significant / not significant” labels.

Private insurance is an informative marker—not proof of protection

Among 1,249,332 respondents with recorded private health insurance status, holders had higher unadjusted survival. The adjusted model then used 116,477 complete records and stratified by survey year.

05

Actuarial interpretation

What the model supports
  • Smoking is a strong mortality marker in both the focused and broader samples.
  • PHI is useful for portfolio monitoring and segmentation diagnostics.
  • Socio-economic and lifestyle variables should be considered together.
  • Sample coverage and missingness belong in model governance, not a footnote.
What requires restraint
  • PHI should not be treated as a direct causal pricing factor.
  • BMI may proxy structural disadvantage and should not be used alone.
  • Small cancer subgroups cannot support aggressive underwriting decisions.
  • Any rating use requires jurisdiction-specific legal, ethical and fairness review.

06

What I corrected

Unknown is not “No cancer”

The original rule labelled a person cancer-free when any available organ-specific field said “No.” The rebuild preserves uncertainty and defines the breast cohort from a direct positive response.

Lifestyle definition matched the narrative

The report described normal BMI plus never smoking, but the code also counted overweight respondents. The new code uses one definition throughout.

Model denominator disclosed

The PHI section described 1.25 million observed records next to an adjusted model fitted on 116,477 complete cases. Both samples are now labelled at the point of use.

Formal PH check added

The Cox model now includes a Schoenfeld-residual test. Age showed evidence of non-proportionality in the small cancer model, so its single hazard ratio is presented cautiously.

Calendar cohorts controlled

The broad PHI model stratifies by survey year so respondents entering in different years do not share one baseline hazard.

Causal language removed

Observed PHI and lifestyle differences are described as associations. Claims about healthcare access, treatment or behaviour would need a stronger identification design.

07

How I would explain it in an interview

60-second explanation

“I analysed linked health-survey and mortality records using Kaplan–Meier curves and Cox proportional hazards models. The most important part was not just fitting the model—it was auditing the cohort. I found that the original cancer rule treated some unknown responses as negatives, the lifestyle definition was inconsistent, and the PHI model’s complete-case sample was much smaller than the descriptive cohort. After rebuilding it, current smoking remained a strong mortality marker: a hazard ratio of 2.86 in the breast-cancer cohort and 2.51 in the broader model. PHI was associated with an 18% lower adjusted hazard, but I would not call that causal because of selection and unmeasured confounding. The project taught me to connect survival modelling with data quality, model assumptions and ethical risk classification.”

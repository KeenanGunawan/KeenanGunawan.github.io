Mortgage Protection Insurance Pricing

A transparent pricing model linking improving mortality, competing policy decrements and a declining mortgage balance to premiums, profit margins and stress outcomes.

Products3 mortgage options
Target12% profit margin
MethodsMakeham · UDD · Profit testing
View reproducible pricing code

01

The pricing problem

A mortgage protection policy pays the scheduled loan balance at the end of the month of death. Premiums stop on death, lapse, prepayment or maturity. The model therefore needs mortality, loan amortisation, policy persistence, expenses and profit to move together month by month.

Death benefitOutstanding balanceMeasured after the month’s mortgage repayment
Competing exitsDeath · lapse · prepaymentConverted from independent to dependent monthly rates
Price definitionNPV profit / EPV premiumPremium solved to equal a 12% target
What makes it actuarial

The premium does not simply scale with the initial loan. It reflects when deaths occur, how quickly the balance amortises, how many policies remain in force and when expenses and commissions are paid.

03

Monthly pricing algorithm

  1. 1
    Convert mortality to monthly death probabilities

    Integrate the hazard over each month and convert the independent death, lapse and prepayment rates into dependent UDD decrements.

  2. 2
    Project the in-force population

    Start at one policy and reduce the active proportion each month by all three competing decrements.

  3. 3
    Amortise the loan

    Calculate the level mortgage repayment and roll the scheduled balance forward after interest and repayment.

  4. 4
    Project expected cash flows

    Collect premiums in advance, recognise fixed expenses and commissions, and pay the balance-weighted death benefit at month-end.

  5. 5
    Solve the premium

    Exploit the linearity of premium income and commission to solve directly for a 12% NPV-profit-to-EPV-premium margin.

  6. 6
    Stress the frozen price

    Hold the baseline premium fixed while changing mortality or mortgage rates, then recompute profit margin.

06

What I corrected in the portfolio version

Rate sensitivity was confounded

The original discussion compared B with C, changing the loan by 16.7% at the same time as the rate. The new counterfactual holds loan, term and premium fixed.

Reserve claim did not match the workbook

The report said every negative cash flow was zeroised and only month one required a reserve. The sheets contain 19, 24 and 23 negative gross-cash-flow months for A, B and C, mainly at renewal dates.

Pricing basis made explicit

The reproduced premiums are expected-present-value prices. A separate, internally consistent capital or reserve schedule would be needed before claiming every monthly shareholder cash flow is non-negative.

Hard-coded outputs replaced

The summary and stress outputs in the workbook are largely displayed as values. The portfolio script derives premiums, margins and stresses from assumptions each time it runs.

Independent reconciliation added

The clean implementation matches all three premiums, the selected life-table checkpoints and all six mortality-stress margins.

Minor rounding clarified

The unrounded monthly premium for B is $72.365. Depending on the rounding convention it displays as $72.36 or $72.37, while the required annual result remains $868.

07

How I would explain it in an interview

60-second explanation

“I built a mortgage protection pricing model for three loans. It combines an improving Makeham mortality law, monthly death, lapse and prepayment decrements, and the amortising mortgage balance. I solved the monthly premium analytically so NPV profit equals 12% of expected premium income, then reproduced the result independently in Python: annual premiums of $770, $868 and $1,012. Under a 10% mortality shock, margins fall to roughly 4.3%–4.6%. When I audited the assignment, I found that the original mortgage-rate comparison also changed the loan amount, so I rebuilt it with the loan and term fixed. I also found that the reserve narrative did not match the cash-flow sheets. That review helped me separate a correct expected-value price from the additional capital modelling needed to control monthly negative cash flows.”
